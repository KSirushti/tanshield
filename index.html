Prototype — Round 1 Submission

Toxicity detection that reads Tanglish like a native speaker, not a translator.

TanShield is a multi-task classification framework that scores Tamil-English code-mixed text through three independent prediction heads — Content Type, Target, and Severity — directly detecting hate speech, harassment, toxic language, and cultural sarcasm, instead of one flat offensive/not-offensive label.

The gap

Generic moderation tools weren't built for this register.

Tanglish carries cultural weight that transliteration alone erases. Existing benchmarks label content offensive or not, but stop short of separating what kind of harm it is, who it targets, and how severe it is — collapsing cultural sarcasm, hate speech, and a mildly rude joke into the same bucket.

Binary labels hide the real signal

Offensive / not-offensive tells a moderator nothing about who is targeted or how severe the harm is.

Target and severity get conflated

Treating "who it's aimed at" and "how harmful it is" as one label loses the distinction a moderator actually needs to act on.

Keyword presence isn't proof of target

A caste term in "government should end caste discrimination" isn't caste-targeted harassment — lexicons alone can't tell the difference.

No explanation, no accountability

A single label with no reasoning gives platforms and users no way to contest or understand a decision.

How we'll build it

From candidate signals to validated, multi-task predictions.

Built on the Tamil-English subset of DravidianCodeMix (the HASOC 2021 shared-task data), fine-tuning MuRIL — a transformer pretrained on transliterated Indian-language text.

Lexical trigger ≠ semantic label. A caste term appearing in a sentence doesn't mean that sentence is caste-targeted — "government should end caste discrimination" contains caste vocabulary without being an attack. Seed lexicons only produce candidate labels for Content Type, Target, and Severity; every candidate is validated against human judgment before it becomes a training label.
1

Seed lexicon candidates

Content Type candidates come from toxicity/hate/profanity term lists. Target candidates come from caste/regional/appearance term lists. Severity candidates come from threat-intensity, insult, and teasing markers.

2

Human validation

A hand-annotated subset (minimum 300–500 comments) is scored against both sets of weak labels. Agreement is reported as Cohen's κ plus per-class precision/recall — a number, not a claim.

3

Multi-task fine-tuning

MuRIL is fine-tuned with three independent heads — Content Type, Target, Severity — on the validated labels, so the model learns contextual patterns rather than memorizing keyword presence.

4

Score both heads

Each input receives a distribution across all three heads at once, generalizing beyond the seed lexicon at inference time.

5

Show the reasoning

Token-level highlighting explains each head's score, including why a message was called cultural sarcasm rather than a bare label.

Deliverables

What Round 2 ships with.

A working, judgeable system — not a slide deck.

Fine-tuned three-head model

MuRIL fine-tuned with independent Content Type, Target, and Severity heads on human-validated labels, not raw keyword matches.

Interactive web demo

Text-input interface returning live scores on both axes, tested against inputs judges type themselves — not a fixed script.

Explainability output

Per-prediction token highlighting for each axis, plus a separate tone signal for sarcasm/banter shown as supporting context.

Evaluation report

Precision, recall, and F1 per class on a held-out test split for all three heads, plus Cohen's κ and precision/recall of the weak labels against the hand-annotated validation subset.

Classification framework

Three heads, not one flat label.

Content Type answers what kind of harm it is — restoring cultural sarcasm as a real predicted class. Target and Severity add the prioritization layer moderators need on top of it.

Content type5 classes

Hate speech

Identity-based attacks — caste, religion, region — used to demean or threaten.

Harassment

Targeted, demeaning language aimed at a specific individual.

Toxic / profane

Generic abusive or profane language without a clear identity or individual target.

Cultural sarcasm

Irony or sarcasm rooted in Tamil cultural context — flagged with confidence, not asserted.

Non-offensive

No toxicity, harassment, or hate signal detected.

Target3 classes

None

No individual or group is targeted — general commentary, criticism of ideas or institutions.

Individual

Directed at a specific person, whether affectionate banter or a personal attack.

Group / caste / region

Directed at an identity group — caste, regional, or community-based language.

Severity3 classes

Mild

Low-harm language — teasing, sarcasm, or light criticism with no real intent to harm.

Moderate

Clearly insulting or demeaning language without an explicit threat.

Severe

Threatening, dehumanizing, or identity-based language causing serious harm.
